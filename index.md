<p>Hi there! I'm a Ph.D. student in Biostatistics at the <a href="https://www.umass.edu/public-health-sciences/" target="_blank">UMass Amherst School of Public Health and Health Sciences</a>, with relevant experience in statistics, data science, and healthcare.</p>

<p>My research involves Biomarker discovery, Multiomics, Bayesian networks, and Clinical trials, where I develop and apply statistical methods to make sense of complex biomedical data, with specific applications in cardiovascular diseases and other chronic health conditions. I'm also excited about the role of statistical methods in clinical trials and pharmaceutical research, especially how innovative trial designs can inform drug development and improve healthcare decisions.</p>

<p>My work spans both academic research and the pharmaceutical industry. At UMass Amherst, I work with large-scale proteomics data to better understand potential sex differences in stroke mechanisms. I've also had the opportunity to explore healthcare, where I worked on evaluating Bayesian designs for fixed and adaptive Phase II/II clinical trials.

<p>Beyond research, I'm involved in mentorship programs focused on career and professional development involving undergraduate and pre-college students from diverse backgrounds (see my recent engagement <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/ekoglobalfoundation/about/the-team?authuser=0" target="_blank">here</a>). Before joining UMass Amherst, I earned a B.Sc. in Statistics from the <a href="https://www.knust.edu.gh/" target="_blank">Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology</a>, Ghana.</p>

<p>A little fun fact about me: I once developed my weekly cleaning schedule using R and emojis, just because I think even chores deserve a good data structure! Right?</p>
